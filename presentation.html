Tripura-X

A vehicle that flies, floats on a hydrofoil and drives on roads, inspired by the Tripura Vimana and planned with today’s technology.

Concept and prototype plan by Arnav Rajpurohit · October 2026

Welcome. Tripura-X is a single-seat tri-mode vehicle concept. The name nods to the Tripura Vimana, a craft the Vaimanika Shastra describes as moving on land, water and in the sky. This deck separates the inspiration from the engineering: what is real today, what a first prototype should be, and how to build it in stages.

The idea

One platform, three modes

01

Fly

Eight electric rotors lift one pilot on short hops of about 10 to 15 minutes. Wheels and floats double as landing gear.

02

Surf

A retractable hydrofoil lifts the hull out of the water, so it skims on roughly 4 to 8 kW. It is an eFoil, scaled up.

03

Drive

Hub-motor wheels and folding rotor arms for low-speed roads, in the 25 mph low-speed-vehicle class.

Design rule: short hops in the air, long range on water and road. Battery energy decides everything.

Each mode borrows from a proven product: multirotor personal aircraft for flight, eFoils for water, and low-speed electric vehicles for roads. The innovation is the integration on one hull, not a new physics trick.

In a NASA study, the 20-minute reserve took 64% of onboard energy.

So Tripura-X is a short-hop machine: 10 to 15 minutes in the air, and an hour or more on water and road.

Sources: PwC Strategy&, eVTOL battery outlook; NASA Lift+Cruise study, Vertical Flight Society Forum 2026

At 200 Wh/kg, the NASA study vehicle drops from a 75-mile design range to 15 miles. That is why the plan targets short hops and plans for battery upgrades as 400 to 500 Wh/kg cells reach production.

Think of the hull as a small boat that happens to carry rotors, wheels and a foil. The hull gives buoyancy and crash structure, the battery sits low for stability, and every extra mode adds weight, so each mode keeps its hardware as simple as possible.

Mode 1 · Air

Eight rotors, one pilot

Item Target (engineering estimate)
Gross mass About 318 kg: 228 kg vehicle plus a 90 kg pilot
Rotors 8 rotors of 1.0 m, disc loading near 500 N/m²
Hover power About 80 kW electrical
Battery 14 kWh at 200 Wh/kg pack level, about 70 kg
Flight time About 9 minutes of hover, 12 to 15 minutes with forward flight
Failure goal Controlled landing after the loss of any one motor

Method: momentum theory, ideal power = T^1.5 / √(2ρA) with T = 3,120 N, then 65% rotor efficiency and 85% motor-and-controller efficiency.

This is the hard mode. Hover needs about 80 kW for a 318 kg craft, which is why the battery is the biggest single item in the weight budget and why flights stay short. Every number here is a first-pass estimate to be replaced by thrust-stand test data.

Modes 2 and 3 · Water and road

Proven technology, scaled up

Water: hydrofoil

  • A 1,550 cm² front wing lifts 318 kg at about 7 m/s.
  • Foil-borne cruise near 9 m/s draws about 4 to 5 kW. Plan for 8 kW.
  • Production eFoils already use 5 kW motors for up to two hours.

Road: low-speed trike

  • Two hub motors of about 3 kW each.
  • Top speed of about 40 km/h, the 25 mph low-speed class.
  • Rotor arms fold so the vehicle stays narrow.

Sources: Waveshark E-Foil 2 Sport specifications; CNN report on Alef Model A

The foil numbers use simple lift and drag estimates: lift equals half density times speed squared times area times lift coefficient, and a lift-to-drag ratio of about 12. A real foil needs CFD or tank testing. Production eFoils prove the motor-and-foil package works at rider scale.

Mode switching

Drive, float, foil, fly

01

Drive

Road mode at up to about 40 km/h to a ramp or beach. Rotor arms stay folded.

02

Float

The hull enters the water and the wheels retract into the hull.

03

Foil

The foil strut drops, the pod accelerates and the hull rises clear at about 7 m/s.

04

Fly

Take off from a ramp or pad after the pre-flight check. Arms unfold, foil and wheels retract.

The flight computer allows a mode change only at a safe speed and after sensor checks.

The sequence is deliberately one-directional on each leg. Taking off from open water is hard because of spray and wave motion, so the plan uses ramps and pads first and treats water takeoff as a later milestone.

The battery and the three mode systems are the heavy items. Every kilogram added raises hover power, so each mode’s hardware has to earn its place. Mass figures are targets for a first design and will move as parts are chosen.

Safety

Built to fail safely

Ballistic parachute

Rated for 318 kg and budgeted at 12 kg.

Battery safety

Cell monitoring, thermal barriers and a sealed, isolated pack.

Redundancy

Eight motors, two flight computers and two power buses.

Water cut-off

A dead-man switch stops the drive if the pilot falls, as on eFoils.

Road guards

Folded arms and guarded rotors while driving near people.

Test ladder

Thrust stand, tether, sandbag, then a trained test pilot.

Never put a person on an untested aircraft. The test ladder is the core safety practice: every step proves one risk before the next step adds another.

Build roadmap

Three phases, one risk at a time

Phase 1 · 0 to 3 months

Subsystem rigs

A 5 kg unmanned quad with floats and wheels, plus an RC foil boat. Prove each mode alone.

US$2.5k to 5k

Phase 2 · 3 to 9 months

Integrated demonstrator

One unmanned hull that drives, floats, foils and flies. Check weight-class rules first.

US$25k to 60k

Phase 3 · 9 to 24 months

Single-seat prototype

A manned craft flown by a trained test pilot under the aviation authority’s experimental rules.

US$150k to 400k+

Cost ranges are rough planning estimates, not quotes.

The ladder is the point. Phase 1 can start this week with off-the-shelf parts. Do not move to a manned phase until the unmanned demonstrator has completed repeated mode transitions without incident.

Phase 1 is hobby-scale hardware. It is enough to test floating, wheeled landing, foil lift on a small boat, and the control logic for mode changes. Wear eye protection and test over open ground or calm water away from people.

Rules and risks

What can stop the project

Rules to check

  • Drone registration and weight-class rules, such as DGCA in India or the FAA in the US.
  • Manned flight needs special authority. Alef’s Model A earned an FAA special airworthiness certificate limited to exhibition and R&D.
  • Road use under a low-speed vehicle class, about 25 mph.
  • Boating registration and life jackets on water.

Top technical risks

  • Battery energy caps flight time near 10 to 15 minutes.
  • Weight creep from carrying three sets of hardware.
  • Foil stability during the lift-off hump.
  • Spray and water ingestion when flying from water.

Source: CNN report on Alef Automotive’s Model A certification

This is not legal advice. Rules differ by country and change quickly, so confirm with the national aviation authority, road transport authority and local water-safety rules before testing anything larger than a hobby drone.
